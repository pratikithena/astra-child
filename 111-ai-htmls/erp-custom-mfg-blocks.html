FAQ

Questions people ask about ERP for custom manufacturing

Short answers on matching ERP to your production model and keeping orders connected from quote to close.

What should a custom manufacturer look for in ERP?

Look for a connected flow between customer requirements, estimates, BOMs and revisions, purchasing, inventory, production, delivery records, and job costing. The right fit depends on your products, order variation, and existing systems.

Can ERP support a quote before the final BOM is ready?

Some systems can support preliminary estimates. Ask vendors to show how assumptions, preliminary quantities, labor, supplier inputs, exclusions, approvals, and revisions move into the approved order.

How should we compare ERP vendors?

Give every vendor the same realistic order scenario. Score demonstrated workflow fit, change control, costing, integrations, implementation, security, and usability.

What production models should we include in ERP selection?

Map make-to-stock, make-to-order, configure-to-order, and engineer-to-order work. A manufacturer may use more than one model across product families.

How do we test engineering change control?

Revise a component after purchasing has started. Check approvals, current BOM version, open orders, inventory, shop floor work, schedule, and job cost.

What should an ERP implementation cost estimate include?

Assess licenses, configuration, custom development, migration, testing, training, integrations, support, and internal staff time. Confirm the scope with each vendor.

Key takeaways

The short version

  • Start with real order workflows, not a feature list.
  • Test a BOM change after purchasing begins and trace its operational and cost impact.
  • Compare implementation effort and support needs alongside software fit.

Match your production model

Choose ERP around how each product is made

Custom manufacturers may stock standard products, build to order, configure a platform, or engineer a solution for one customer. Define those models before deciding what an ERP must demonstrate.

Custom manufacturing ERP systems integrate quoting, engineering, procurement, inventory, production, delivery, and finances for variable product lines. Their success depends on real-time access to accurate requirements, BOMs, revisions, allocations, schedules, and job costing across an order’s lifecycle.

Key operational strategies include:

  • Make to Stock (MTS): Focuses on forecasting, inventory accuracy, automated reordering, and scheduling.
  • Make to Order (MTO): Focuses on order-specific MRP, job costing, and timely fulfillment.
  • Configure to Order (CTO): Focuses on feature compatibility, dynamic pricing, configuration rules, and BOM generation.
  • Engineer to Order (ETO): Focuses on project tracking, custom BOMs, revision control, purchasing, and cost containment.

Organizations must adapt these baselines to custom workflows. For example, in ETO, order specs precede final BOMs, making end-to-end traceability from customer request to engineering design essential, as seen in SAP’s engineer to order example.

Follow one order

Compare ERP approaches with a real order

Purpose built industrial ERP, general purpose manufacturing ERP, and highly customized ERP can all be valid options. Compare the actual workflows, implementation plan, and support model rather than relying on category labels.

Choosing an ERP system involves balancing trade-offs between purpose-built industrial, general manufacturing, and customized solutions.

To evaluate vendors, test them using a standardized RFQ scenario during demos:

  • Estimation & Order Conversion: Record assumptions, BOMs, labor, quotes, approvals, and margins, then convert quotes to active orders while retaining data access.
  • Engineering Change Management: Revise a component mid-procurement to evaluate version control and system-wide impacts.
  • Procurement & Tracking: Track a long-lead item and simulate a schedule delay to test real-time visibility.
  • Financial Reconciliation: Compare initial estimates against actual spending, commitments, billing, and remaining costs.

Score the evidence

Evaluate proof, implementation, and support

Use a weighted scorecard, but keep critical workflow, security, or integration gaps visible even if the overall score is high.

For every requirement, record whether it is standard, configured, custom-built, dependent on another product, or outside the ERP. List the evidence: demonstration, documentation, reference, or future commitment. Include licenses, configuration, custom work, migration, testing, training, support, and internal staff time in the cost view.
